High Yield OAS — 10 Year
271 bps
HY at 271bps is near post-GFC lows. COVID spike to 1,090 in Mar 2020 and oil-crisis spike to 840 in Feb 2016 show how fast spreads can blow out. Current tightness reflects strong risk appetite, but limited room for error.
IG Corporate OAS — 10 Year
77 bps
IG at 77bps is near 25-year tights. COVID blowout to 375bps in Mar 2020 was the sharpest widening since the GFC. Spreads have compressed steadily since late 2022 as credit quality held up better than feared.
BBB Corporate OAS — 10 Year
100 bps
BBB at 100bps — the largest IG bucket and key "fallen angel" risk zone. BBB spreads spiked to 475bps during COVID. Current levels leave minimal buffer for downgrades. Watch for spread widening if recession fears grow.
2s/10s Treasury Spread — 10 Year
+27 bps
Curve un-inverted in late 2024 after the deepest inversion (-105bps in mid-2023) since the early 1980s. Currently at +27bps — positive but well below the +90bps long-term average. The 2022-2024 inversion historically preceded recessions, though timing varies.
Credit market risk assessment: Spreads are historically tight across all credit tiers — HY at 271bps (-31% vs avg), IG at 77bps (-49% vs avg), BBB at 100bps (-46% vs avg). This reflects strong risk appetite but leaves minimal cushion for macro shocks. The 2s/10s curve has normalized but remains flat. All-in yields still attractive (HY ~6.8%, IG ~5.3%) due to elevated Treasury base rates. Key risk: a credit event or recession scare could reprice spreads 200-400bps wider in weeks, as seen in Mar 2020.

Layer 2 — Structural Forces & Disruption
These secular themes operate independently of the business cycle and can overwhelm cyclical signals for affected sectors.
AI & Compute Buildout ACCELERATING
The scale: Hyperscaler capex hitting $725B in 2026, up 77% from 2025. Amazon ($200B), Microsoft ($190B), Alphabet ($175-185B), Meta ($115-135B). This is the largest private infrastructure buildout in history.

What it means: Massive demand for GPUs (NVDA), custom silicon (TSM, MRVL), power infrastructure, cooling systems, and data center REITs. AI software monetization (MSFT Copilot, GOOGL AI Overviews) in early innings. Enterprise adoption still <10% penetrated.

The risk: ROI questions are growing. $725B in capex needs to generate returns. If AI revenue growth disappoints in late 2026/2027, the capex cycle could decelerate sharply — creating a semiconductor downcycle.

GLP-1 & Healthcare Transformation ACCELERATING
The scale: GLP-1 market projected at $82B in 2026, growing to $150B+ by 2030 and $190B by 2035. ~20M Americans now on branded GLP-1s, expected to reach 30M by 2030. Oral formulations launching in 2026 will massively expand access.

The disruption: This isn't just a pharma story. JPMorgan estimates $30-55B in annual food & beverage revenue destruction by 2030. GLP-1 users consume 21% fewer calories and spend 31% less on groceries. Snack food, fast food, alcohol, and bariatric surgery are all losing demand. Meanwhile, health insurers may benefit from reduced obesity-related costs long term.

Competition heating up: Novo Nordisk, Lilly lead, but Amgen, Viking Therapeutics, Roche, AstraZeneca, and Pfizer all have candidates. Pricing pressure is inevitable as competition grows.

Energy Security & Middle East Crisis ACUTE RISK
The crisis: Iran conflict has caused the largest supply disruption in oil market history (IEA). Strait of Hormuz restrictions affecting 27% of global crude and 20% of LNG trade. Brent averaged $86/bbl in 2026 (up from $69 in 2025), peaking above $100. Qatar LNG capacity hit — 17% reduction, 3-5 year repair timeline.

Macro impact: Energy prices projected +24% in 2026 (World Bank). This is the primary driver of CPI re-acceleration to 4.2%. European energy crisis 2.0 unfolding (TTF gas doubled). Import prices surged 1.9% MoM in May — the pipeline for more inflation ahead.

Duration risk: Unlike 2022 (Russia-Ukraine), this disruption affects the Strait of Hormuz choke point. Resolution timeline is uncertain. Even a ceasefire won't quickly restore Qatar LNG capacity.

Framework Summary
The cyclical regime alone would say: go defensive. But the structural forces tell a more nuanced story. Two of the most powerful secular tailwinds in decades — the $725B AI infrastructure buildout and the $80B+ GLP-1 revolution — are happening during a stagflationary macro environment. This creates a market where sector selection matters far more than market-level calls.

The highest-conviction positions combine cyclical AND structural tailwinds: Energy (stagflation hedge + ME crisis), Healthcare (defensive + GLP-1), Utilities (defensive + AI power demand), and select Industrials (reshoring + aero aftermarket).

The trickiest call is Tech. The macro screams vulnerability (25x P/E, rising rates), but the AI capex cycle is unprecedented. The resolution: be selective. Own the profitable AI infrastructure layer (NVDA, TSM, MSFT, GOOGL) and avoid speculative/unprofitable names. If the AI ROI narrative cracks, even the good names will correct — but they'll recover fastest.

The biggest risk to monitor: Middle East escalation. It's simultaneously the most likely, most impactful, and most uncertain variable. An oil spike to $120+ would push the regime dot deeper into stagflation and force defensive positioning across the board.
